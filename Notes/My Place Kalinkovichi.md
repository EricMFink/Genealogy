This small place spread between the forests and the swamps of Polesie. A small town, about two hundred and fifty families. Surrounded by dozens of Belarusian villages, Kalinkovichi tremblingly kept their identity.

All earnings of local Jews were closely associated with nearby villages. Only a few families were wealthy - forest traders, wheat, officials. Half of the townships were “bypassers of villages”: mostly tailors and shoemakers, who walked through the villages and sewn local clothes, boots, and everything they ordered. Among them were small merchants - bondiggars, they sold haberdash goods or exchanged them for skin, flax, fur. On Sunday, spa rank, taking with them tools or goods, without forgetting the talit, Tfilin and the prayer book (Sidur), they left their families. For a whole week they worked in villages, ate aches adamant bread brought from home, stay away from the tref of the villagers. They worked, prayed and slept in peasant huts, and only by Saturday they returned home to his wife and children.

The second source of income was the bazaar day. At the entrance to the place there was a church - a tall building painted in red and green. On Saturday, Sunday and holidays, hundreds of peasants on the car went to the bazaar. On a long bazaar street there were food rows, trays with equipment, fabrics and clothes. Craftsmen - tailors, shoemakers, hatters - were preparing for the bazaar for a week. All products are waiting for buyers. Buyers rush between peasant guises, everything is bought - chickens, eggs, sheep's wool, everything that a peasant farm can give. Having sold all the brought, the men were going to the local shops to buy "from lace to hat": oil, lubricant for carts, salt, sugar, nails, sickles and braids. Place-bearing children these days turned around their bench parents, looking after the goods, saving it from unclean visitors. The timber dealers were looking for mercenary workers in a crowd of loggers for deforestation, taken from the landowner for rent.

Near the town there was a railway lane Pinsk-Gomel. The Kalinkovichi station also served the county town of Mozyr, which was located a few kilometers south. Around the railway station was a settlement of officials and workers serving the station. The station was also given to local Jews: the drivers delivered cargo to Kalinkovichi and Mozyr, the loaders unloaded the cars, the shopkeepers provided all the necessary residents of the station and passengers.

### Berko Governor

In the area of the station held his tavern Berko-Gubernator. He was so called because he was familiar and got along with everyone – from simple workers to the management of the station, postal servants, gendarmes and other representatives of the authorities.

In his institution, locals came to skip a bitch and bite delicacies of the wife of Berko-Guberner Feige-Rearzl, known in the district with a particularly tasty “gephate fish” and other dishes, hotly revered by the head of the local gendarmerie. The Berko-Governor, being a retired soldier of the Russian army and fluent in Russian, easily settled the conflict situations, using his special gift of eloquence, wisdom, the ability to “give on the paw” to whom it is necessary, and in some cases the strength of the fist. He was a consultant and adviser to the town Jews, Ishuvnikov (Jews who lived in the villages), tenants of inntal yards and lands. He was approached when the threat of ownership and leases were threatened.

When Berko learned about the pogroms or robbery attacks, he, having enlisted the tacit support of the local gendarmerie, he convened Jewish cabmen and movers. Particularly distinguished the family of Nahum Gomon - the father of three sons, known strongmen who could easily pull the prey out of the carriage and mark the robbers so that they never want to engage in robbery again.

Berl Rabinovich _вероятно__(probably_ the _same Berko-Gubernarus –_ _approx._ _N._ _Эстис_Estis) headed the detachment of local self-defense in the tense days of 1905, and the same during the Russo-Polish war, when the Bulak-Balakhovich gangs staged a pogrom in the town. He saved many by the strength of his hand, by the power of speech and wisdom. He died in 1938. Blessed his memory will be blessed.

Behind the market, where local Jews in the sweating of their lives earned themselves and their family, there were public buildings. Three synagogues stood in the form of a triangle and had a joint courtyard, the so-called "Hacer Beit Acnet". At the head of this complex stood the Old Synagogue. To the right of it was the Small Synagogue, and the New One on the left. In the old synagogue, the time of the foundation of which none of the locals could remember, prayed ordinary citizens - artisans and small traders. In the small synagogue, old Jews prayed, not burdened with caring for earnings, as well as young Jews - avrrechim (_studying the Torah and Jewish laws_), living in the care of his father-in-law. In this synagogue, they were in no hurry to start a prayer in rank, but they taught the Talmud before and after prayer. In the new synagogue, majestic and beautiful, with new furniture, prayed wealthy Jews, rich merchants, as well as enlightened towns, who read [Ha-Tsfira](https://eleven.co.il/jewish-literature/periodical-press/11083/).

Most of the inhabitants of the town belonged to the Hasidic court of Stolin. However, he also lived in the town of Admor rubb Baruch-David Tversky, a descendant of the reb Nahum from Chernobyl.

Those close to the Rebbe Baruch-David Hasidi enjoyed the light of his sermons during the feast at the end of the Sabbath, he was able to wisely and accessible to interpret the events taking place in the world, entrepreneurs and Jewish public figures from remote towns turned to him for advice. Among his Hasidim was also my father and teacher Dov-Berl Rabinovich, known for being able to please the hearts of the Hasidim with tunes, Hasidic dances and “Cossack”.

On the holiday of Simhat Beit Ha-Shoava (after Sukkot), the child danced with the Torah scroll surrounded by all the inhabitants of the town. Near the house of the rub lived the reb Schlomoo Menaker, one of the members of the famous Gloyberman family from Pinsk. He was a prominent public figure and sage who understood everything that happened. His house was distinguished by pronounced Zionist sentiments. The son of Reb Shilomo, [Yehoshua Gloyberman](https://eleven.co.il/zionism/from-balfour-to-state/11211/), was a member of the kibbutz Yagura and one of the senior commanders of Haganah. He was killed by an Arab gang on the way to Jerusalem at the beginning of the Israeli independence war. His grandson is writer Zvi Krol.

The third spiritual authority of the place was Rav Mordechai (Ben Shlomo) Shapiro (~1867–1943), a graduate of the Volozovskaya Yoshiva. In his youth, he was also a merchant, but on the advice of his wife, Rebetzin Zipora, who wanted her husband to inherit his father's seat and be a rabbi, he went to study with yeshiva and received a rabbi's attestation, while she took all the troubles in the house and income. He was part of commercial circles, as he knew how to judge people, had the skills of buying and selling. Even the Russian peasants _(apparently, the Belarusian – belisrael)_ preferred his rulings to the decisions of the state court.

In the town there were heders (schools of primary education for boys), groups for the study of the Torah and Talmud, but Kalinkovichi gained fame thanks to the Hebrew writer, Zionist and public figure Yosef-Haim-Khaim Dorozhko. He lived a life full of torment and suffering.

Dorozhko (1869-1919) was endowed with rare outstanding abilities that turned out to be unclaimed and not fully realized in the harsh conditions of life of an abandoned Jewish town of Eastern Europe.

While still a teenager, he was very seriously ill, his body and legs remained paralyzed for life. About 30 years - until his death - he spent in bed. He lived in his sister's house on a long Bazarnaya Street. The leadership of the community instructed his sister’s husband to collect a meat tax on the condition that the brother-in-law and his family take care of the sick parent-writer. The shopping house of Vysotsky from Moscow also sent Dorozhko a monthly allowance in the amount of 25 rubles.

According to his views, Dorozhko was a religious man, and at the same time an educated and ardent Zionist. Without leaving the house, he wrote articles in Jewish newspapers, sent letters to writers and national public figures. The envoys of Zionist organizations who visited Kalinkovichi, including [Chaim Weizmann](https://eleven.co.il/state-of-israel/history-of-state/10865/) (he mentioned Dorozhko in his notes as a “righteous man from Kalinkovich”), [Zalman Epstein,](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%AD%D0%BF%D1%88%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%B9%D0%BD,_%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BD) [Yehuda-Layb Kaganovich](https://belisrael.info/?p=255) talked about the indelible impression that Dorozhko made.

His room was the workroom of Zionists, educated Jews and youth circles of the town. He was brought on a stretcher to the synagogue and at the meetings of the Zionists. Later, they were able to buy a special cart for him, and we young people, harnessed it when he wanted to be in a pine forest near the town. In this forest there were summer camps of the rich from Kiev and Kharkov, who came to breathe with resin aroma.

In this grove, the youth gathered around the cart, sometimes honored guests joined them, and all together they were engaged in mesmerizing conversations with the writer. The road to the grove passed through the sands. On one side of the road was a Jewish cemetery, on the other - Christian. The cart was not easy to carry, because the writer who was in it weighed not so little, but he treated us with sympathy; when he saw that we were tired, he asked to rest a little. While we tried to catch our breath, he shared with us long-standing stories, talked about the traditions of the town.

The name Dorozhko is associated with an attempt to establish a new Jewish school in the town with teaching in Hebrew. In 1909, [Zalman Epstein](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%AD%D0%BF%D1%88%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%B9%D0%BD,_%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BD) published a pamphlet in which he called for trying to make Hebrew a lively spoken language, following the example of how it was done in Eretz Yisrael and in several towns of Russia. The path was inspired by this idea and began to spread it immediately among the inhabitants of the town. He urged them to give all their children to the Hebrew kindergarten and the Hebrew school, the foundation of which will be the first step to root Hebrew as a spoken language. He prepared the text of the decision, which was signed by the rabbis, gabai synagogues, heads of public institutions and the most authoritative homeowners. According to the decision, they pledged to support the initiative, which was gradually supposed to lead to the fact that Hebrew from the past will become a living spoken language of the community.

Following this decision in early 1911, a Hebrew kindergarten and a Hebrew school were opened in Kalinkovichi. Teachers and educators were graduates of the Grodno courses, including Jacob Baram, Abraham-Aba Slutsky, Esther Kleiner and Sarah Mendeleeva, may their memory be blessed. This educational center lasted two years and closed due to lack of funding and quarrels between teachers, one of whom even wrote a complaint that the training program does not meet state requirements.

Failure greatly upset Yosef-Haim. He even inclined to leave Kalinkovichi and move to a big city. For many years he had a great idea - to translate the Talmud into Hebrew, but the hard reality did not give these dreams to come true.

Under the influence of Dorozhko, some young men went to the yeshiva in the city of Lida, which Rav Reines founded (see _About the Lida_ “new type of yeshiva” [here –](https://belisrael.info/?p=7184) belisrael), and returning to the town, they opened the Hebrew library. In the evenings and at the end of Saturday, young people gathered in the library and talked about pressing problems. Among them, I recall a Jewish Kommissar who disappeared in the crucible of the revolution, Beinish Minevich, who later repatriated to Israel and worked in the field of education.

Several natives of Kalinkovichi became known in the writing world, among them Israel-Meir Gorelik (1873-1956), a teacher and writer who settled in Argentina, Shlomo Simon (born in 1895), a Yiddish writer and folklorist who lived in the United States, Zalman Telesin (1907-1996), wrote in Yiddish in the USSR.

The First World War began. Trains were passing through Kalinkovichi that transported Jewish refugees from the front line. Jewish activists of the town and members of charitable organizations were on duty at the railway station, meeting the refugees and providing them with everything necessary for the next road: food, clothes.

At the beginning of 1917, the tsarist government was overthrown. On the streets of the town began to flutter red flags, the Jews joined the city council.

The community of destinies and a sense of freedom overwhelmed the hearts of the inhabitants, regardless of nationality and religious affiliation. The change of power was also excised by the Zionist movement. Jewish youth took part in heated discussions in the synagogues between Bund activists and Zionist parties, in preparation for the elections. The share of the younger generation of the place in all this was great. Suddenly, the alarming winds blew up. Increasingly, calls for pogroms were heard, the civil war was gaining momentum. The Bolshevik government captivated the hearts of the youth with the ideas of the communist revolution.

Representatives of the youth received responsible positions in the management of the city, thus reducing the influence and strength of the “local Jewish capitalists”. Most Jewish youth continued to do Zionist activity, but many young Jews joined the Komsomol.

At the end of the 1910’s, power changed every six months. The power of Hetman Skoropadsky was replaced by the occupation of the Polish army, and with it and its allies -- the gangs of Bulak-Balakhovich, which rampaged, robbed, and killed. Many people fell victim to the brutality of these scoundrels who were an abomination in the district in the 1920s.

Riots and robberies did not bypass the town of Kalinkovichi. One night, when gangs of Balakhovites broke into the place, ten Jews who tried to resist were brutally murdered. For several weeks in the small town raged bandits, as no help arrived from Red Army units. Immediately the peasants of the district rebelled against the Soviet regime. The youth of the town took up arms and organized self-defense units -- sometimes even at the cost of their own lives they protected other places from robbery and violence.

Most of the participants in self-defense groups were members of the Zionist movement, who continued Zionist activities under the new government. Many of them tried to illegally cross the border, and those that were delayed by the border guards were sent into exile, on servitude. Those who were lucky, settled in Israel and enthusiastically took up for any work -- they drained the swamps of the Jezre'el valley, founded the kibbutzim, worked as part of the "workers' detachments".

The place of Kalinkovichi continued to live its life under the burden of Soviet power until the summer of 1941, when the hordes of the fascist army occupied the place and destroyed almost all the remaining Jewish population. Only a few managed to evacuate and avoid bitter fate. Many of them joined the partisans, among them my brother Yosef Rabinovich (may God avenge his death) -- he was hanged in the city square by the Gestapo scum. Many wars scattered at different ends of the Soviet Union.

The place of Kalinkovichi was and disappeared.

-- **Zalman Bagarav (Rabinobich)**, (Kalinkovichi, 1902 - Ashkelon, 1983)
